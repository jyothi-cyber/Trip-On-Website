Everything you should know about Ubud

Trip On Editorial
Updated July 2026

Ubud is the cultural heart of Bali — a lush highland town surrounded by rice terraces, sacred forests, and ancient temples. Known worldwide for traditional arts, craft villages, and the iconic Monkey Forest, Ubud offers an immersive blend of spirituality, nature, and Balinese heritage that draws travellers seeking more than just beaches.

History:

Ubud's name derives from the Balinese word "ubad" meaning medicine, reflecting its ancient role as a centre of healing herbs and traditional remedies. Situated in the Gianyar Regency, the town grew from a cluster of village compounds into a powerful artistic capital over many centuries.

In the 19th century, Ubud was the seat of the Sukawati royal family, who played a central role in promoting Balinese arts. The presence of the royal court drew painters, dancers, musicians, and sculptors, turning Ubud into a living gallery of traditional Balinese culture.

During the 1930s, Western artists like Walter Spies and Rudolf Bonnet settled in Ubud, collaborating with local artists and founding the Pita Maha arts collective. Their influence helped Ubud gain international recognition, attracting intellectuals, writers, and explorers who wrote extensively about its beauty.

Temple Inside:

Ubud's most celebrated temple complex is the Monkey Forest Sanctuary, home to over 700 long-tailed macaques and three ancient Hindu temples dating to the 14th century. The moss-covered stone gates and forest canopy create an otherworldly atmosphere unlike anywhere else in Bali.

The Ubud Palace (Puri Saren Agung) sits at the centre of town and continues to function as the official residence of Ubud's royal family. Every evening, its inner courtyard becomes a stage for traditional Legong and Kecak dance performances illuminated by torchlight.

Further into the hills lie the Goa Gajah Elephant Cave and the Yeh Pulu rock relief carvings — ancient sacred sites where stone-carved demons guard bathing pools and centuries-old inscriptions tell stories of Balinese mythology.
