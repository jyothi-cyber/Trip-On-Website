Everything you should know about Lombok

Trip On Editorial
Updated July 2026

Lombok is Bali's quieter, wilder neighbour — an island of dramatic volcanic peaks, pristine white-sand beaches, and the deeply traditional Sasak culture. Mount Rinjani, Indonesia's second-highest volcano, dominates the skyline and draws trekkers seeking crater lake panoramas, while the southern coast delivers world-class surf breaks and near-empty beaches.

History:

Lombok's history is layered with the influences of the indigenous Sasak people, the Balinese Hindu kingdom of Karangasem, and the Dutch colonial administration. The Sasak people practise a unique blend of Islam and pre-Islamic animist traditions called Wetu Telu.

In the 18th and 19th centuries, the Balinese dominated western Lombok from their stronghold in Mataram, introducing Hindu temples and cultural traditions that still survive today at Pura Lingsar. The Dutch defeated the Balinese in 1894, absorbing Lombok into the colonial Dutch East Indies.

Modern Lombok began attracting surfers and adventurous travellers in the 1990s, drawn by uncrowded breaks along the Kuta coastline and the challenging ascent of Rinjani. Today it balances low-key tourism with authentic island life.

Sacred Sites:

Pura Lingsar is the most important religious complex in Lombok, uniquely celebrating both Hindu and Sasak Wetu Telu faiths within the same compound. The temple hosts the annual Peresehan festival and the Perang Topat ceremony.

The Segara Anak crater lake sits at 2,000 metres inside Mount Rinjani's caldera, regarded as sacred by both Hindus and Muslims. Pilgrims make the arduous three-day trek to bathe in its hot springs and cast flower offerings into its turquoise waters.

The traditional Sasak villages of Sade and Ende preserve architectural traditions unchanged for centuries — houses built on raised bamboo platforms with thatched alang-alang grass roofs, and floors polished with a mixture of clay and buffalo dung.
