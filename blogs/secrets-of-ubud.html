Secrets of Ubud: Culture and Nature

Leo Vikram May 25, 2026 9 min read 15.3K views Ubud, Bali

Overview

Uncover the hidden treasures of Ubud, the cultural heart of Bali. From sacred temples nestled in jungle valleys to traditional art galleries and organic cafes, this guide reveals Ubud's best-kept secrets that most travellers never discover. Go beyond the well-worn tourist path and experience authentic Balinese culture through spiritual ceremonies, traditional dance performances, and meaningful interactions with local communities.

"Ubud rewards the curious traveller. Every narrow lane that looks like it leads nowhere eventually opens onto a temple courtyard, a rice paddy, or a family compound where incense burns and offerings are laid at dawn."

Ubud's Hidden Gems

  • Secret waterfall trails off the beaten path
  • Traditional healing ceremonies and yoga retreats
  • Local artisan workshops and craft studios
  • Organic farm-to-table dining experiences
  • Monkey Forest sanctuary at early morning
  • Sacred springs and holy water temples

The Secrets Revealed

Hidden Waterfalls

Beyond the well-known Tegenungan, Ubud hides several secret waterfalls that locals know but guide books rarely mention. Tukad Cepung, accessible via a narrow canyon walk, has mystical rays of light piercing through rock crevices at mid-morning — a photographer's dream that looks almost supernatural. Tibumana, a serene twin-tiered waterfall perfect for swimming, is rarely crowded even on peak tourist days.

1

Tukad Cepung Waterfall

A cave waterfall where shafts of sunlight pierce through rock at 10–11am. Best visited on clear mornings. 30-minute walk from the car park through rice fields.

2

Tibumana Waterfall

Twin waterfalls in a lush ravine north of Ubud. Swimming-friendly with clear pools. Almost unknown to tourists — you may have it entirely to yourself.

3

Kanto Lampo Waterfall

A stepped cascade over volcanic rock perfect for wading and photography. Best in the morning before tour groups arrive.

Sacred Temples Beyond the Crowds

While Tirta Empul is famous, smaller temples like Gunung Kawi Sebatu offer equally powerful spiritual experiences without the crowds. Set in a lush valley with spring-fed pools, the holy water rituals here are conducted with an authenticity that the busier sites can no longer provide. Entering in the late afternoon, when tour groups have departed, and witnessing a local family's purification ceremony is profoundly moving.

The Pura Kehen temple in Bangli — just 30 minutes from Ubud — is one of Bali's most architecturally magnificent temples yet receives only a fraction of the visitors of Besakih. Its 11-tiered meru tower and ancient waringin fig tree are extraordinary, and the silence within its courtyards is a rare gift.

Traditional Healing

Ubud is renowned for its traditional Balinese healers (Balian). These practitioners use ancient techniques combining energy work, therapeutic massage, and spiritual counselling rooted in lontar manuscripts written over centuries. Many visitors report profound healing experiences — both physical and emotional — that conventional medicine had not addressed.

Ask your accommodation to recommend a trusted local Balian rather than relying on tourist agencies. The experience is deeply personal and best approached with genuine openness and respect for the tradition.

Art Villages and Craft Studios

Away from the main shopping streets, local artisans quietly create extraordinary traditional crafts. The silversmith village of Celuk (15 minutes south) houses multi-generational families where intricate filigree jewelry is made by hand using techniques unchanged for three centuries. Mas village produces stunning wood carving — the studios here are working workshops, not tourist showrooms.

The Batuan painting style — dense, dark, highly detailed compositions depicting ceremonial life — is still practiced by a small community of painters in Batuan village. Visiting their studios and watching them work is one of the most memorable cultural experiences in all of Bali.

Organic Farm-to-Table Dining

Ubud's farm-to-table movement is among the most genuine in Asia. Sari Organik, accessed by walking through a narrow path between rice fields, serves produce harvested from its own farm that morning. The experience of eating food literally grown metres from your table, surrounded by working rice paddies with gamelan drifting on the breeze, creates a connection with the land that is unlike anything else.
